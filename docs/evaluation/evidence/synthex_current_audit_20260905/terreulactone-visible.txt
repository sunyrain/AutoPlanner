SYNTHEX'S STRATEGY
Synthesizing the highly substituted decalin core via a biomimetic Lewis acid-catalyzed polyene cyclization of an acyclic terpene precursor to stereoselectively establish the bicyclic framework.
⤢
FULL ROUTE
8 steps · fragments converge left → right · click a structure or step number to jump to that step
ROUTE SUMMARY
The route employs a highly convergent and biomimetic strategy to assemble the complex polycyclic target. The key feature is a Lewis acid-catalyzed polyene cyclization that rapidly constructs the highly substituted decalin core from an acyclic terpene precursor, directly addressing the user constraint. Subsequent steps logically elaborate the core by appending a coumarin moiety, forming the pyran ring via dehydrative etherification, and finally installing the 4-methoxyphenyl group via a Suzuki-Miyaura cross-coupling.
ROUTE CRITIC
This route features a highly strategic and elegant biomimetic polyene cyclization to rapidly assemble the complex decalin core. The subsequent steps logically build the remaining rings and append the peripheral groups in a convergent manner. While the key cyclization and some regioselective couplings carry inherent risks, the overall step economy and strategic brilliance make this a very strong synthetic plan.

TOP RISKS
#1 The biomimetic polyene cyclization (Step 6) may suffer from low yield or incorrect stereochemistry due to competing elimination pathways.
Extensive screening of Lewis acids, temperatures, and solvents to optimize the cascade and trap the intermediate carbocation efficiently. → jump to step 2
#2 Alkylation of 4-hydroxycoumarin (Step 4) may favor O-alkylation over the desired C-alkylation.
Use specific Lewis acids (e.g., Sc(OTf)3) or water-based conditions known to favor C-alkylation of 4-hydroxycoumarins. → jump to step 4
#3 Dehydrative etherification (Step 2) may lead to epimerization at the tertiary alcohol, giving a mixture of diastereomers.
Carefully control reaction time and acidity, or explore alternative milder cyclization conditions to ensure thermodynamic control favors the desired epimer. → jump to step 6
STEREOCHEMISTRY

The route relies heavily on substrate control in highly congested environments. The polyene cyclization and the dehydrative etherification carry significant risks of forming undesired diastereomers.
